# Hypothesis: Stationary distribution is sensitive to high-mass row perturbations

## Claim

Perturbations to transition matrix rows corresponding to high-π states cause disproportionately large shifts in the stationary distribution π, while perturbations to low-π rows have negligible effect.

## Context

- **Pattern:** Self-Healing / Adaptive (14)
- **Related example:** `examples/self_healing_nodes`
- **Motivation:** Architectural components that are frequently visited (high stationary mass) are critical "hot spots". Errors or tuning adjustments in their transition logic impact the entire system's steady-state more than adjustments to rarely-visited components. Understanding this sensitivity helps prioritize engineering effort on the kernel entries that actually matter.

## Variant definitions

| Variant | Label | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| A | High-π perturbation | A small δ perturbation is applied to a row with high stationary mass in the `self_healing_nodes` Variant A kernel. |
| B | Low-π perturbation | A small δ perturbation is applied to a row with low stationary mass in the same kernel. |

*Variants are generated by selecting rows from the same base kernel (Joint kernel J from self_healing_nodes Variant A) based on their stationary mass.*

## Variable kernel entries

| Kernel | Entry (from, to) | Variant A value | Variant B value | Why this encodes the claim |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| J | High-π row (i, j) | P(i,j) + δ | P(i,j) | Tests sensitivity of the most "popular" state. |
| J | Low-π row (k, l) | P(k,l) | P(k,l) + δ | Tests sensitivity of a "rare" state. |

*The perturbed rows are renormalized to maintain row-stochasticity. δ = 0.01.*

## Predictions

| Metric | Expression | Predicted direction | Why |
|--------|-----------|--------------------|----|
| Stationary shift | \|Δπ\|₁ | A > B | High-π rows have higher leverage on the global balance equation πP = π. |
| Sensitivity ratio | empirical / theoretical | A ≈ B | Both should match the first-order approximation dπ/dP. |

## Verdict rule

The claim is supported if Variant A (high-π row perturbation) consistently yields a total variation distance |Δπ|₁ at least one order of magnitude larger than Variant B (low-π row perturbation), and both empirical shifts align with the theoretical sensitivity formula.
